High risk

Natural hazard & insurance risk

liquefaction zone, old building — insurance is very expensive
  • Flood — FEMA Zone X (low risk)
  • Earthquake — located in a high liquefaction zone; 1924 building likely unreinforced masonry or non-ductile concrete
  • Fire — no wildfire risk, but high arson frequency in the area
  • Other — extreme heat island; proximity to I-10/I-110 adds air toxins
  • Insurance — commercial property insurance in Skid Row is exorbitant; Midnight Mission self-insures partially through donations. For a developer converting a building, costs would be prohibitive without subsidies
Cost warningA major earthquake would cause severe structural damage; earthquake insurance, if available, would be extremely expensive due to the combination of old building + liquefaction zone.
CA Geological Survey · FEMA · USGS
In flux

Future developments

intervention and displacement pressure at once
  • Inside Safe — Mayor Bass's program has cleared blocks around San Pedro & 6th into hotels; many return. Open question: does it push more people toward the Mission?
  • 250-unit PSH filed — 6-story permanent supportive housing + ground-floor services on the city lot at 610 S San Pedro; debated as relief vs. concentration
  • Surge ops — periodic "Operation Healthy Streets" sweeps when crime spikes
  • Arts District pressure — southward expansion rebranding "Downtown's Edge" raises displacement risk
DiligenceNeighborhood Council and CD-14 minutes surface housing and sweep decisions months ahead; parcels searchable in ZIMAS.

Owner / rental insider — adjacent lofts

"urban pioneer" marketing vs. the reality one door away
  • One Santa Fe (0.3 mi) — luxe lofts, rooftop pool, direct view of encampments; ~$3,000–$5,000/mo. 2024 HOA minutes show resident petitions for private perimeter security; recurring garage break-ins
  • Barker Block (0.5 mi) — HOA contends with package theft and garage break-ins; some residents active in improvement groups
